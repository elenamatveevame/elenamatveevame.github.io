A companion you can read at a glance

Two directions for the Home screen, drawn to Apple’s iOS 26 guidelines, and the four states the companion moves through. The character is a placeholder. These sketches cover structure and behavior, not the final render.

Two directions, one set of components

Both phones run the same state machine and share every component. Play a request to watch it move through all four states and change the day, then flip the iPhone settings to see Dark Mode and the accessibility versions.

iPhone settings

In either phone, tap the mic, a suggestion or the keyboard. After a change, try Undo. In B, drag the sheet up or tap the list button.

Six decisions behind the screens

What each choice does for the person holding the phone.

  • The next event leads

    One wide card with a countdown sits on top. Tasks, inbox and tonight shrink to tiles, so the companion gets the space they free up.

  • Talk or type

    The mic sits in the thumb zone and the keyboard is one tap away, for meetings, trains and people who would rather not speak. A typed request skips Listening.

  • Suggestions on an idle screen

    Two chips built from the day show what the companion can do, so a new user never starts at a blank mic.

  • Thinking names the step

    A short line like “Checking Sam’s evening” turns a 1.5 s wait into visible progress.

  • Changes can be undone

    When the companion edits the calendar or contacts someone, the reply says so and offers Undo for 8 seconds.

  • Clear turns

    Your words appear by the mic, the companion’s above its head. Screen readers hear each reply once, as a whole sentence.

Built on Apple’s iOS guidelines

Both directions follow the Human Interface Guidelines for iOS 26, so they feel native on day one and cost less to build.

  • Liquid Glass for controls, material for content

    The dock, suggestions and the state pill float on Liquid Glass. Cards that carry information sit on the regular material, so text stays readable over the scene. Inside the sheet, buttons use a plain fill, so glass never stacks on glass.

  • System text styles

    Body 17, Headline 17, Subheadline 15, Footnote 13 and Title 2 for numbers. Every label is a text style, so it scales with Dynamic Type.

  • Real iPhone geometry

    Drawn at 393 × 852 pt, the iPhone 16 screen, with 59 pt and 34 pt safe areas, 16 pt margins and 44 pt minimum touch targets.

  • A native sheet

    The day uses two detents and a drag indicator. At the small detent it floats 8 pt from the edges with corners concentric to the screen, then goes edge to edge at the large one.

  • System colors and SF Symbols

    Text uses label and secondaryLabel. States use systemOrange, systemTeal, systemPurple and systemPink, so Dark Mode and Increase Contrast adapt without extra work. Every icon maps to an SF Symbol.

  • Settings people already chose

    Dark Mode, Reduce Transparency and Reduce Motion change both phones live. Try the switches above the phones.

Four states, each with a job

Motion tells the user what the assistant is doing. Each state changes the tone, the pose and one signature movement, and each one has a reduced-motion version.

Idle

systemOrange #FF9500

Starts when
The app opens, or a reply finishes.
Motion
Breathes on a 4 s loop, ease-in-out. Blinks every 4 to 6 s.
Reduced motion
Still pose, eyes open.

Listening

systemTeal #30B0C7

Starts when
The user taps the mic or says the wake word. Typed requests skip this state.
Motion
Leans in over 240 ms. Glow and ring follow the mic level, smoothed over 60 ms.
Reduced motion
Static ring and a Listening label.

Thinking

systemPurple #AF52DE

Starts when
700 ms of silence ends the user’s turn.
Motion
Three dots orbit every 1.6 s and a step line says what it is checking. Both wait 300 ms, so quick replies never flash.
Reduced motion
Dots hold still in an arc. The step line stays.

Speaking

systemPink #FF2D55

Starts when
The first audio chunk arrives.
Motion
Mouth follows the audio level. A ripple leaves every 470 ms and fades over 1.4 s.
Reduced motion
No ripples or mouth movement. Captions stay on screen.

Why information sits on material

Body text needs 4.5:1 contrast with whatever sits behind it. Over the window and shelves that changes from pixel to pixel, so every card that carries information sits on the regular material: a 30 pt blur under a 66% fill that turns solid with Reduce Transparency. Controls use the clearer Liquid Glass because they hold one symbol or a short label. The companion stays the only thing that sits on the scene directly.

Built to hand off

Every piece maps to a native building block and every value to a token, so the spec and the build stay in step. Names are SwiftUI for iOS 26; Flutter and React Native have close equivalents.

Components

  • CompanionStageRive or Lottie view. state: idle, listening, thinking, speaking. Reads accessibilityReduceMotion.
  • NextCard.background(.regularMaterial, in: .rect(cornerRadius: 26)). Fonts .headline and .subheadline. Solid grouped fill inside the sheet.
  • StatTileSame material, radius 22. Value in .title2 bold, label and detail in .footnote.
  • SpeechBubble.thickMaterial, text in .body. Step line while thinking. Undo is a plain button in the tint color, shown for 8 s.
  • SuggestionChip.buttonStyle(.glass), 44 pt capsule. Hidden while the companion is busy.
  • DockSide buttons .buttonStyle(.glass), 50 pt. Mic .buttonStyle(.glassProminent), 64 pt, tinted by state. Turns into stop while the companion speaks.
  • DaySheet.presentationDetents([.height(276), .large]), .presentationDragIndicator(.visible), .presentationBackgroundInteraction(.enabled).
  • Haptics.sensoryFeedback(.impact(weight: .light)) on the mic, .success when a change lands.
  • SF Symbolscalendar, checklist, envelope, cloud.rain, mic.fill, keyboard, list.bullet, clock.arrow.circlepath, arrow.up, arrow.uturn.backward, stop.fill.

What I’d ask next

The answers decide how far the design goes into the character itself and which direction fits.

  • Do the companion characters already exist, as 3D assets or renders?

    If not, I’d write the specs for a 3D or motion artist and use AI tools for early concepts.

  • How will the companion render: Rive, Lottie, Unity or video loops?

    It sets how states blend. Rive can blend live, while video loops need planned transition clips.

  • Native iOS and Android, Flutter or React Native?

    Blur and translucency cost different amounts on each stack, so the widget spec should match it.

  • What do people open the app for most often?

    Tasks and calendar point to A. Talking to the companion points to B.
