Salad: payroll advance for Novacard

A demand test for Novacard's payroll advance in Mexico: would people move their salary before the full product existed? Launched in one month against a three-to-six-month estimate.

Role
Product Manager
Context
Novacard, via ProSense
When
Aug 2026 · 1 month
Outcome
Demand tested in 1 month instead of 3–6. 17.5% of paid visits became completed applications.
Visit the live site ↗

The question

Payroll advance is decided in minutes, and trust is the only real barrier. Novacard needed proof that people would switch: completed applications from cold paid traffic, with no human contact.

What I did

Product manager from discovery to launch: the PRD from stakeholder interviews, design direction with a rationale for every decision, prototypes on the existing design system, post-launch analytics. The PRD itself is in the AI-Assisted PRD Delivery case.

What shaped the design

  • Advance capped at 10,000 MXN, which shaped the calculator
  • LFPDPPP, Mexico's data protection law, limited what the form could ask
  • Full-funnel attribution with UTM, click_id and AppsFlyer OneLink
  • Several teams and integrations, first scoped at three to six months

Decisions

Hero → calculator → form: people see their own number before we ask for any data.

Real app screens answer the fear "will the money actually arrive?" before anyone types their details, and the last screen shows the funds received, not a generic confirmation.

A real customer replaced a stock crowd photo.

What happened

After launch, four variants ran on paid traffic, one URL each (/39, /49, /79 and /99). In the first 11 days:

Variant Visits Started Completed Conversion
/391,73848028416.3%
/491,42938323016.1%
/791,46948127919.0%
/991,53947628718.6%
Total6,1751,8201,08017.5%

The best-performing variant converted at 19.0% against 16.1% for the weakest, an 18% relative lift, found by testing rather than guessing.

17.5% of paid visits became completed applications, and 59.3% of people who started the form finished it.

SALAD (Adelanto de Nómina) · Dummy Site · One-Pager

Author: Elena · Created: August 2026 · Updated: August 2026 · Tier: L (Large)
Problem
Novacard doesn't know whether people in Mexico's open market are ready to move their salary to Novacard for Salad (Adelanto de Nómina), or whether there's strong demand for this kind of credit line. Launching the full product without validation is a high financial and operational risk.
Who it's for
Employed Mexicans earning 9,600–19,000+ MXN, customers of BBVA, Banorte, Santander, Citibanamex, Banco Azteca, Scotiabank, and BanCoppel.
Goal and metric
Test the hypothesis that users are willing to move their salary to Novacard. Visit → completed form conversion ≥ X%; the threshold is an Open question for the product owner (client) before launch.
Solution
A landing page with a hero offer, a limit calculator (min(salary × 40%, 50,000 MXN)), a 4-step application form, and a confirmation screen. No OTP and no hand-off to the app: applications are collected with UTM and click_id into a table and tagged in the LMS.
Out of scope
A/B testing, CRM integration, automatic export to the analytics system, onboarding existing customers, phone OTP verification, hand-off to the mobile app, application scoring and underwriting.
Key dependencies
Novacard LMS (LMS team), marketing analytics (client's marketing), legal (LFPDPPP), design (Figma), tech lead (stack and domain), ad platforms.
Success criterion
Visit → form conversion and funnel data quality are good enough to make an informed call on launching the full product.
Top risks
(1) The conversion threshold that would confirm the hypothesis isn't defined. (2) The LFPDPPP legal copy isn't ready. (3) There's only a prototype so far, not final designs.
Open questions
Conversion threshold (product owner, client), LFPDPPP consent copy (legal), how leads are tagged in the LMS (LMS team), tech stack and domain (tech lead), who analyzes the results (product owner).

SALAD (Adelanto de Nómina) · Dummy Site · PRD

Author: Elena · Created: August 2026 · Updated: August 2026 · Tier: L · Links: designs in Miro and Figma (final version is an open question), tickets TBD, analytics dashboard TBD

1. Context and Rationale

Problem. Novacard plans to enter Mexico's payroll market with Salad (Adelanto de Nómina, a credit limit based on salary). Only banks serve this segment; fintechs don't compete there yet. Before launching the full product, demand has to be validated: are users willing to move their salary from their bank to Novacard in exchange for benefits? The pain today is that there's no data confirming demand, and launching without validation is a high financial and operational risk.

Evidence. Facts (confirmed):

  • Average salary in Mexico is about 19,000 MXN, minimum about 9,600 MXN (source: client meeting, 19 June 2026)
  • Market norm: credit issued doesn't exceed 40–50% of the customer's salary
  • Target source banks: BBVA, Banorte, Santander, Citibanamex, Banco Azteca, Scotiabank, BanCoppel

Hypotheses (not confirmed):

  • Users are willing to move their salary to a fintech in exchange for benefits
  • Novacard's offer (limit, debit card, cashback, and yield) is attractive enough to convert

Every funnel event is sent to the dataLayer through dataLayer.push, and the hypotheses are tested on that data.

Goal and success metric. Test the hypothesis that Mexican users are willing to move their salary to Novacard.

MetricTargetStatus
Visit → completed form conversion≥ X% (threshold TBD)Open question Product owner (client), before launch
Step conversion through the funnel (1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → submit)Diagnose drop-offsNeeds event tagging

Non-goals (deliberately out of scope):

  • A/B testing landing page variants
  • CRM integration
  • Automatic data export to the analytics system
  • Onboarding existing Novacard customers
  • Phone OTP verification
  • Sending users to the mobile app after they apply
  • Application scoring and underwriting

Strategic fit. Salad is the first step in Novacard's entry into Mexico's payroll market. The landing page is a demand-validation tool: its results decide whether the full product is worth launching. Market potential is rated high, and fintechs aren't present in it yet.

2. Solution

Main user flow:

StepAction
1The user lands on the page from an ad (UTM + click_id)
2Sees the hero with the Adelanto de Nómina offer of up to 50,000 MXN and taps “Check My Limit”
3Calculator: enters their salary (slider, 5,000–30,000 MXN) and picks a pay frequency. The system shows the limit: min(salary × 40%, 50,000 MXN)
4Sees the limit and taps “Apply” to go to the form
5Form step 1, Basic info: name, email, phone, city, Novacard customer status
6Step 2, Work: employment type, employer, tenure, industry, how the salary is paid
7Step 3, Income and Nómina limit: salary, pay frequency, current bank, desired advance amount
8Step 4, Transfer and consents: willingness to move the salary, motivation, two required checkboxes
9“Join the Pilot” → confirmation screen “Application submitted”
10Data, UTM, and click_id are saved to a table; the lead is tagged Salad in the LMS

Functional requirements:

  • Landing page: hero, “How it works”, “Why Nova” (5 benefits), calculator, FAQ
  • Calculator: 5,000–30,000 MXN slider, pay frequency, real-time limit using min(salary × 40%, 50,000). CAT 46% / 58% / 87%, service fee 56 / 70 / 105 MXN per 1,000 MXN (weekly / twice a month / monthly)
  • 4-step form with a progress bar, Back and Continue, and a final “Join the Pilot”
  • Client-side validation of every field before moving to the next step
  • After submitting, a confirmation screen with no redirect to the app
  • Form data, UTM, and click_id saved to a table, with CSV export
  • Leads tagged with a tag or field in the Novacard LMS
  • Analytics events at every funnel step (see Data and analytics)

Design. Designs are linked in Miro and Figma; the final version is an open question, and only the SALAD_dummy_v3 prototype exists for now. Changes to the current app screen in the hero: Tarjeta de crédito → Adelanto de Nómina, and the Wallet icon is removed. The first form screen is merged with the calculator and cut down to full name and phone; the other steps are unchanged. Every “Apply” button leads to the form block, with no separate path through the calculator. The FAQ is unchanged.

States and edge cases:

StateBehavior

Business rules and logic:

  • Limit formula: min(salary_MXN × 0.40, 50,000), calculated client-side in real time
  • Service fee: 56 / 70 / 105 MXN per 1,000 MXN (weekly / twice a month / monthly)
  • CAT (46% / 58% / 87%) is a mandatory regulatory disclosure on the calculator and in the FAQ
  • Both consent checkboxes in step 4 are required to submit
  • “Existing NovaCard customer?” is required; in the MVP it affects analytics segmentation, not routing

4. Delivery and Verification

Acceptance criteria (Given / When / Then). Calculator:

  • Given a salary of 20,000 MXN / When the user moves the slider / Then the limit is 8,000 MXN
  • Given a salary of 30,000 MXN / When the user moves the slider / Then the limit is 12,000 MXN (30,000 × 40%; the 50,000 MXN ceiling can't be reached within the current 5,000–30,000 slider, see the open question below)
  • Given a salary of 5,000 MXN / When the user moves the slider / Then the limit is 2,000 MXN

Form validation:

  • Given “Full name” is empty / When the user taps Continue / Then the field is highlighted and moving on is blocked
  • Given an email without “@” / When the user taps Continue / Then a validation error shows
  • Given neither consent is checked / When the user taps “Join the Pilot” / Then the button is disabled or an error shows

Saving data:

  • Given the form was submitted / When the data is saved / Then the “Application submitted” confirmation screen shows, with no redirect to the app
  • Given the user arrived with ?utm_campaign=salad&utm_source=fb / When the form is submitted / Then those values are saved in the record
  • Given a click_id in the URL / When the form is submitted / Then the click_id is saved

LMS tagging:

  • Given a Salad lead is saved / When the record reaches the LMS / Then it's tagged “Salad” and the client's product owner can filter by it

Scope by phase:

PhaseContent

Rollout and rollback. A separate subdomain or domain, not connected to the main Novacard database (decided with the tech team on 4 June 2026). Launch behind a feature flag, with ad traffic only after QA. Rollback: turn off the flag or change the DNS record, with recovery under 30 minutes and no impact on the main site. Data collected before a rollback is kept and can be exported.

Launch analytics. Results are reviewed at 7 days (operational) and 14 days (the main decision on the hypothesis). Continue if conversion is at or above the threshold, which is still an Open question for the product owner (client). Stop if form save errors exceed 5%, or if conversion is below 50% of the threshold after 7 days. Who analyzes the results is an Open question.

UX Design

eir.ie self-service redesign

Eir, an Irish telecom, wanted older customers to pay bills and manage plans online instead of calling support.

Role
Senior UI/UX Designer
Context
eir, Irish telecom
When
3-month engagement · web + mobile
Outcome
Paying a bill, checking usage and managing a plan each became one clear path for older customers, with every failure state designed, from a declined card to an expired one.
Visit the live site ↗

What eir needed

  • Usability: easy for people with little digital experience.
  • Accessibility: reduced vision, dexterity and digital literacy.
  • Engagement: an account people want to use.
  • Time: three months to design and ship.
  • Remote: all research and testing ran remotely under COVID-19.

Try it: paying a bill on mobile

Paying a bill had to be easy. These are the real screens, wired like the Figma prototype.

Tap anywhere to see what's clickable.

What to try

  1. Let the account load: the customer is greeted by name, not a blank screen.
  2. Scroll the account page, then tap Make a payment.
  3. On the summary, tap Edit to pick another card, or Add another payment method to add one.
  4. Tap Pay now, then Close. The menu at the top right opens My bill and My plan.

Why it works for older customers

  • One number first: the balance, what it includes, and when the direct debit goes out.
  • One clear action per screen, with large tap targets and plain labels.
  • The amount and the card can be changed before paying, and nothing is charged without a final Pay now.
  • The confirmation says what happens next: the payment can take up to 3 business days to show.

The same flow on desktop

On desktop, payments open in a dialog over the account, so nobody loses their place. Open My bills or pay from the balance card.

Click anywhere to see what's clickable.

  1. Research & discoveryStakeholder meetings, remote interviews with older users via Lookback, a competitive analysis.
  2. Ideation & wireframingLow-fidelity wireframes refined with stakeholders and early users.
  3. Prototyping & testingHigh-fidelity prototypes tested remotely before build.

Research with older customers

Separate desktop and mobile questionnaires, and task flows documented step by step (SIM activation, the Amazon Prime Video offer), so engineering knew what "done" meant.

Every state, not just the happy path

Every state was designed: payment success and failure, a card verified, approved, declined or expired, plus alerts, offers and bill variants.

Volunteer Project · Process Improvement

Cleaning schedule for a shelter in The Hague

A shelter in The Hague for mothers and children affected by domestic violence: six floors, and residents share the cleaning of the common areas. As a volunteer I proposed a simpler way to run the monthly cleaning schedule, then designed and built it. The app is now being tested in daily life.

Role
Volunteer: product, UX and build
Context
A shelter in The Hague, 6 floors
When
Oct 2026 · pilot running
Outcome
Staff and residents took it up with enthusiasm. Residents now pick and swap their own turns.

How it worked before

Staff built the schedule by hand every month in a Word table and posted it in a WhatsApp group where only staff could write. Nobody was asked which days they couldn't clean, swaps happened by word of mouth in the kitchen with no record, and residents had no way to reply.

What the app had to respect

  • Residents speak different languages: the app works in Dutch, English and Arabic
  • No accounts and no personal data: you pick your room, not your name
  • Staff aren't technical and must stay in control, able to fix anything by hand
  • No budget

What I did

I noticed the friction, suggested the change to the team, and did the product and UX work and the build myself, with Claude Code. From the first idea to a working pilot took one day.

Four decisions

  • A Google Sheet as the database. Staff see and fix everything in a table they already know, and it costs nothing.
  • No accounts, pick your room. No friction in three languages. A swap needs the other room to accept, and every change is logged.
  • Swaps go both ways. You hand over a turn and take one back, so nobody ends up with extra work.
  • Calendar reminders instead of phone numbers. Turns go straight into the phone's calendar, without collecting personal data.

Try it

A working copy of the app with made-up rooms and a fake backend, so nothing you do here reaches the real schedule. Pick a room to see your turns, mark days you can't clean, or ask for a swap. To try the staff side, choose I'm staff and enter the demo code 12345.

The staff side

The same four steps every month: invite residents, collect the days they can't clean, generate the schedule in one click, publish. In a test with the real rooms, the generator made no conflicts, gave every room 8 to 10 turns, and never put a room on two days in a row.

Screens with changed room numbers. Shown in English; the app also runs in Dutch and Arabic.

Where it is now

The pilot is running. The team took it up with enthusiasm, and residents are starting to use it: they're glad they can pick and swap turns themselves, without the back-and-forth there was before. Results will follow after the first full month.
